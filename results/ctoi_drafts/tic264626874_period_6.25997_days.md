### Ellipsoidal variation

A companion of mass M2 raises tides on the star, which gives a flux variation at half the orbital period:
A = alpha (M2 / M1) (R1 / a)^3 sin^2 i, with alpha about 1.1. With a/R* 6.57, a 1 Jupiter-mass planet gives about 1.5 ppm,
13 Jupiter masses give about 20 ppm, and 0.1 solar masses gives about 160 ppm.

The fit removes the transits and the phase-0.5 window, fits one polynomial trend per continuous segment, and fits
-cos(4 pi phase) (ellipsoidal), with or without -cos(2 pi phase) and sin(2 pi phase). The error is the standard deviation
of the same fit at 200 to 300 wrong periods (0.6 to 1.6 times the period, random epoch), which includes red noise.

| light curve | trend degree | ellipsoidal amplitude (ppm) | wrong-period standard deviation (ppm) | significance |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| S32 TESS-SPOC PDCSAP | 1 to 5 | 163 to 184 | 44 to 61 | 3.0 to 3.8 sigma |
| S32 TESS-SPOC SAP | 1 to 5 | 226 to 253 | 74 to 96 | 2.5 to 3.4 sigma |
| S98 QLP SAP | 2 | 164 | 321 | 0.5 sigma |

The sign is the sign of an ellipsoidal variation, with the minima at the conjunctions.
The S98 QLP light curve has a scatter of 3716 ppm out of transit, against 512 ppm for S32, so it does not test the signal.
